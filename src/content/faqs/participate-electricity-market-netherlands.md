If you have an electricity supply contract, you already participate as a **retail customer**. Going further can mean changing when you use electricity, selling surplus solar production, or allowing a provider to adjust a home device in exchange for a reward. Start with your household's existing equipment and the consumption you can shift without disrupting daily life.

This guide covers **households in the Netherlands**, with sources checked on **10 October 2026**. Device compatibility and programme availability depend on the provider.

## Which route fits my situation?

| Your situation | A practical starting point | What you gain access to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Household able to shift consumption | Compare dynamic electricity contracts | Retail prices that change with the market |
| Rooftop solar owner | Arrange exports with your supplier and register the installation | Payment or settlement for exported electricity |
| Owner of a controllable EV charger, battery, or other flexible device | Find a compatible aggregator or supplier flexibility programme | Rewards for allowing agreed adjustments |
| Solar owner who wants to share surplus electricity | Ask whether your supplier offers energy sharing | Sharing arrangements with another household under the supplier's conditions |

Some providers combine smart charging with an additional flexibility programme. For example, Tibber requires Smart Charging to be enabled for its EV Grid Rewards programme. Ask how a provider coordinates the two and calculates the combined benefit. [Tibber: Grid Rewards eligibility](https://support.tibber.com/hc/nl/articles/48351672784401-Grid-Rewards-voor-elektrische-auto-s)

## How can I save by changing when I use electricity?

A **dynamic contract** passes changing electricity prices through to your bill. Check the supplier's pricing interval, smart-meter requirements, purchasing fees, fixed charges, and export terms. ACM explains that dynamic electricity tariffs can change by quarter-hour, hour, or day, and that a suitable smart meter is needed. Prices can rise as well as fall. [ACM's explanation of electricity tariffs](https://consument.acm.nl/elektriciteit-en-gas/wat-betaal-ik-voor-mijn-energie/vaste-variabele-leveringskosten-energie)

Use the tariff to schedule flexible consumption: charge an EV before departure, or run a suitable appliance during cheaper intervals. Compare the **price you actually pay**, including applicable taxes and fees, rather than a wholesale price chart alone. ACM distinguishes the price per kWh from fixed supply charges, network charges, and taxes. [ACM: components of your electricity costs](https://consument.acm.nl/elektriciteit-en-gas/wat-betaal-ik-voor-mijn-energie/vaste-variabele-leveringskosten-energie)

For an **invented example**, shifting 10 kWh from €0.30/kWh to €0.20/kWh saves €1.00 if those are your complete consumption tariffs and the energy requirement stays unchanged. Fixed charges are unchanged. This is a bill saving; there is no separate flexibility payment in this example.

In the **day-ahead market**, an auction sets prices for electricity delivered the following day. [Nord Pool: the daily day-ahead auction process](https://www.nordpoolgroup.com/en/the-power-market/Day-ahead-market/)

If your retail tariff follows those prices, your household responds to an announced price schedule. A scheduled price becoming effective does not itself mean your appliance places an exchange order; that is the distinction between responding to a tariff and trading on the market.

For a concrete charging example, see [how Tibber smart charging connects to electricity markets](../tibber-ev-smart-charging-markets/).

## How do I sell surplus solar electricity?

Tell your supplier that you will export electricity, check the export terms, and register the installation with your grid operator through **Energieleveren.nl**, either yourself or through your installer. Check that your meter records imports and exports appropriately. Export compensation (*terugleververgoeding*) and export charges (*terugleverkosten*) are different items; compare both. [ACM's guide to generating and exporting electricity](https://consument.acm.nl/elektriciteit-en-gas/duurzame-energie/teruglevering-van-elektriciteit)

For eligible households, statutory net metering (*salderen*) continues through **31 December 2026** and ends on **1 January 2027**. Export compensation remains after that date. [Dutch government: end of net metering](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/klimaat-milieu-en-natuur/energie-thuis/salderingsregeling)

From 2027 through the end of 2029, the minimum export compensation is 50% of the electricity supply price excluding energy tax and VAT. **This is not a guaranteed net return:** ACM notes that export charges can exceed the compensation. For a contract spanning 2027, also check any contractual net-metering entitlement. Use those terms when comparing consumption of your solar electricity at home with export. [ACM: exporting after 1 January 2027](https://consument.acm.nl/elektriciteit-en-gas/duurzame-energie/wat-is-salderen)

## Can I share solar electricity with another household?

ACM describes an energy-sharing route available from 2026 where both participants have smart meters and contracts with the same participating supplier. Settlement is per quarter-hour; the recipient pays energy tax and VAT, and the supplier may charge administration fees. Suppliers do not have to offer it. Sharing also leaves less electricity available for net metering while that scheme remains. [ACM: “Energie delen vanaf 2026”](https://consument.acm.nl/elektriciteit-en-gas/duurzame-energie/teruglevering-van-elektriciteit)

## Can I earn rewards with my EV charger or home battery?

An **aggregator** pools electricity consumption or generation from multiple customers for participation in electricity markets. For a household flexibility programme, this can mean coordinating devices across many homes. [EU Electricity Directive 2019/944, Article 2(18): definition of aggregation](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/944/oj/eng)

To explore a programme, ask whether it supports your exact device and electricity contract, how to connect through its supported integration, and when the provider may control it.

For example, Tibber says its Dutch **Grid Rewards** programme can increase, reduce, or pause EV charging and pay a participation reward. Its listed requirements include a Tibber energy contract, a supported car linked in the app, and Smart Charging enabled. Its guidance also says that unplugging before postponed charging is made up usually forfeits price compensation. These are Tibber's own product conditions, not evidence of typical earnings across providers. [Tibber: operation, eligibility, and compensation conditions](https://support.tibber.com/hc/nl/articles/48351672784401-Grid-Rewards-voor-elektrische-auto-s)

Keep two benefits separate when comparing offers: **paying less for consumption** through a charging schedule, and **receiving a reward** for letting the provider change that schedule. Ask for a breakdown showing both, including any additional electricity costs and provider fees.

For a home battery, also include charging and discharging losses and battery wear in your comparison. The Annual Technology Baseline treats round-trip efficiency, degradation, and energy and power ratings as distinct inputs to residential battery assessment. Use your device's specifications for these inputs. [2025 Annual Technology Baseline: residential battery storage](https://atb.nlr.gov/electricity/2025/residential_battery_storage)

A projection of trading revenue alone does not establish your net benefit or payback. Milieu Centraal's household guidance highlights purchase costs and uncertainty about recovering them through bill savings or electricity trading. [Milieu Centraal: “Verdient een thuisbatterij zich terug?”](https://www.milieucentraal.nl/energie-besparen/zonnepanelen/thuisbatterij-zonne-energie-opslaan/)

Ask the provider to name the service behind any claim about “balancing the grid.” ENTSO-E distinguishes responsibility for settling a portfolio's energy imbalances from the provision of balancing services; its Netherlands entry lists product-specific TenneT prequalification for reserve services. [ENTSO-E: balancing roles and requirements, select Netherlands](https://www.entsoe.eu/network_codes/eb/requirements-for-bsps-and-brps-across-europe/)

That distinction means an offer based on imbalance prices alone does not establish participation in a qualified reserve service. For your household, ask whether your device is eligible, what the provider may do with it, and how you are paid.

## What should I check before signing up?

Use this practical checklist to compare offers; the answers depend on the provider's contract:

- **Eligibility:** Does the programme support my exact charger, car, or battery? Do I need to switch electricity supplier?
- **Control:** Who can start, stop, or change its operation? Can I set a departure time or minimum battery charge, and override the controls when needed?
- **Payment:** Is the benefit a tariff saving, an availability payment, an activation payment, or a share of trading proceeds? Which fees and costs are deducted?
- **Availability:** What happens to my payment if I unplug the car, lose internet access, or override a charging command? Can I incur a charge?
- **Compatibility:** Does the arrangement fit my supply contract, connection limits, equipment warranty, and other flexibility commitments?
- **Exit:** How long is the agreement, and what happens to device control and data access when it ends?

## What is a sensible first step?

Collect your recent electricity use, current tariff, and any solar export data. Identify consumption you can move, such as EV charging with several hours of spare time before departure. Compare the resulting costs under suitable contracts, then check whether a compatible flexibility programme adds value after fees. Start with equipment you already own before assessing whether additional hardware pays for itself.
